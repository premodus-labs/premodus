Products are often designed around assumptions that are invisible to the people making them: stable connectivity, abundant support staff, familiar payment rails, or data that is easy to find. Those assumptions shape who can use a product and who is left outside it.

## Build from what is true

Local context includes infrastructure, language, institutional habits, and the cost of failure. It should change the product decisions: what works offline, how information is explained, and how a team can recover from an error.

> A world-class standard does not mean copying a product built somewhere else. It means doing the work carefully enough for the people it serves.

This is also where small teams have an advantage. Close contact with users turns context into a design tool rather than a late-stage requirement.
